FAQ Read This Before You Request a Print

Straight answers about 3D printing, materials, limitations, and what PLA prints are actually good for.

General 3D Printing Questions

What kind of 3D printing is this?

TokerTravPrints uses FDM (Fused Deposition Modeling) 3D printing. This means melted plastic filament is laid down layer by layer to create a finished object.

Why do printed parts have visible lines?

Layer lines are normal in FDM printing. Smaller layer heights (like 0.12mm or 0.06mm) reduce their visibility but increase print time. They never fully disappear without post‑processing.

Are 3D printed parts strong?

Strength depends on design, orientation, and use. PLA prints are suitable for light‑duty parts, display pieces, organizers, and novelties — not high‑stress or safety‑critical uses.

Materials & Filaments

What material do you use?

All prints are made using basic PLA (Polylactic Acid). PLA is chosen for its reliability, clean detail, and consistent results in small‑batch printing.

Why only PLA?

Other filaments like ABS, PETG, Nylon, or Carbon Fiber require different hardware, environments, and safety controls. PLA allows predictable quality without unnecessary complexity.

Food Safety & PLA (Important)

Is PLA food safe?

PLA as a raw plastic can be food‑grade under specific manufacturing and regulatory conditions. However, a 3D printed PLA object is not automatically food safe.

Why printed parts are different

FDM printing creates microscopic grooves between layers that can trap food particles and bacteria. Hobby‑grade printers, nozzles, and filaments are not certified food‑contact systems.

What about cleaning or sealing?

Some people use food‑safe epoxy coatings to reduce surface porosity, but this still does not make a print equivalent to manufactured food‑grade products. PLA also softens at relatively low temperatures and cannot be reliably heat‑sanitized.

Best practice: PLA prints should not be used for repeated or long‑term direct food contact unless you fully understand and accept the limitations.

What 3D Printing Is Best For

  • Display pieces and collectibles
  • Desk and home organizers
  • Controller stands and holders
  • Light‑duty brackets and adapters
  • Hobby and novelty items

Limitations to Understand

  • PLA can deform in hot environments (cars, direct sun)
  • Not suitable for heavy loads or safety‑critical use
  • Surface texture is normal without post‑processing
  • Max print size is 10″ × 10″ × 10″
